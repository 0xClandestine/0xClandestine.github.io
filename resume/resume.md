## Summary

I enjoy making computers do less work.

I'm <strong>self-taught</strong>: business degree, no CS background. I learned to program between lectures from Stack Overflow and docs — in the <span class="term">pre-AI</span> era, so you had to actually <em>figure things out</em>. The entry point was a personal project, automating my trading portfolio to cut risk.

<span class="term">Solidity</span> clicked when I wanted the same on-chain, with Uniswap, Sushi and the early dapps. That's the real appeal of Ethereum: anyone can build a censorship-resistant app that, done properly, lasts.

When <span class="term">gas</span> got expensive, bad design meant an unusable app, so I went deep on optimization — which pulled me into the fundamentals of computer science. <strong>You can't optimize primitives you don't understand.</strong>

Some contracts I've written have held <strong class="big">billions</strong> in deposits at their peak, without loss of funds. The work hasn't changed: design the protocol, then write code that stays correct while real money moves through it.
